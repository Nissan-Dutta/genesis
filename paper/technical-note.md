# How much of the ProteinGym leaderboard is noise?

**Rank intervals, power and robustness for the zero-shot DMS substitution benchmark**

Nissan Dutta. Technical note, draft of October 2026. Code and data provenance:
<https://cursor.com/codebase/nissandutta/genesis>.
The LaTeX version (`paper/technical-note.tex`) compiles to the 2-page PDF. Every number below is generated from
`results/summary.json`; [`numbers.md`](numbers.md) maps each one to its source.

**Summary.** ProteinGym ranks 97 zero-shot models on 217 deep mutational scanning (DMS) assays. Treating its
proteins as a sample and putting confidence intervals on ranks, we find that 3 models cannot be ruled out as #1,
that only 2 of 19 adjacent pairs in the top 20 are statistically distinguishable, and that detecting a gain of
0.01 Spearman over the current leader with 80% power would need about 850 assays (3.9× today).

## 1. Question

ProteinGym [1] is the reference benchmark for protein fitness prediction. Its DMS substitution leaderboard orders
models by a function-group-balanced mean Spearman correlation and reports, for each model, a bootstrap standard
error of its gap to the top model; it reports no uncertainty for the ranks themselves. We ask (i) how uncertain the
ranks are, (ii) what gain the benchmark can detect, and (iii) how far the top of the leaderboard depends on the
metric, the aggregation and assay coverage.

To our knowledge, this is the first analysis to place simultaneous confidence intervals on model ranks in the
ProteinGym DMS substitution leaderboard and to quantify the leaderboard's statistical power and sensitivity to
assay subsets. Statistical ranking is not new to the field: Livesey and Marsh bootstrap rank significance on their
own DMS benchmarks [5, 6], CASP assessors run head-to-head bootstraps [7], and Woolley et al. [4] caution against
over-interpreting ProteinGym's aggregate ranking. Outside biology, Neuhof and Benjamini [2] build leaderboard rank
intervals for TabArena and MMLU, and Chatbot Arena reports bootstrap intervals [8]. Our work brings rank
intervals, power analysis and subset sensitivity together for ProteinGym, with its nested assay–protein–function
structure.

## 2. Data and version

We use ProteinGym GitHub commit `144fe22` (25 March 2026): release v1.3 plus AIDO Protein-RAG and Protriever, 97
models and 217 assays. As in ProteinGym's script, assay scores are averaged within (UniProt ID, function) units
(200 units), units within five function groups (Activity, Binding, Expression, OrganismalFitness, Stability), and
the score is the mean of the five group means.

Our implementation reproduces all 97/97 published averages and 97/97 ranks exactly, and 91/97 published standard
errors at 3 dp; the other 6 lie within 0.0002 of a rounding boundary and change with the bootstrap seed. All 97/97
published averages for AUC, MCC, NDCG and top-K recall also reproduce. Releases themselves drift: from v1.0 to
v1.1 published scores moved by up to 0.011 for ESM-1v (0.158 for Wavenet) [18]; since v1.2, scores of existing
models have not changed (maximum change 0.000).

## 3. Methods

**Estimand.** Let $\theta_m$ be model $m$'s ProteinGym aggregate in the limit of infinitely many units per
function group, treating the benchmark's units as a sample from each group. Its rank $r_m$ is 1 plus the number of models $k$
with $\theta_k > \theta_m$. Our intervals quantify the uncertainty due to which proteins happen to
be in the benchmark. They are not prediction intervals for a model's rank on a *new* assay, which is Neuhof and
Benjamini's target and is much wider.

**Bootstrap.** Exactly as for ProteinGym's error bars, units are resampled with replacement within function groups
($B$ = 10,000), a stratified bootstrap in the sense of [16]. Missing scores are skipped, as in ProteinGym.

**Rank intervals.** Following Mogstad et al. [3], let
$T_{jk} = (\hat\theta_j - \hat\theta_k - \theta_j + \theta_k)/\widehat{se}_{jk}$. The *marginal* interval for
model $j$ takes $c_j$, the 95% bootstrap quantile of $\max_k |T_{jk}|$, and sets the lower bound to 1 plus the
number of $k$ with $\hat\theta_k - \hat\theta_j > c_j \widehat{se}_{jk}$, and the upper bound to $p$ minus the
number of $k$ with $\hat\theta_j - \hat\theta_k > c_j \widehat{se}_{jk}$; it covers $r_j$ with probability at least
95% for each $j$. The *simultaneous* interval uses one critical value from $\max_{j,k} |T_{jk}|$ and covers all
ranks jointly; its step-down version [17] recomputes the critical value over the comparisons not yet rejected. The
95% best-model set keeps $j$ unless some model beats it at a one-sided max-t critical value, so it contains the
true #1 with probability at least 95%. We report marginal single-step and simultaneous step-down intervals. The
percentile interval of bootstrap ranks is shown for contrast; it has no guarantee and fails near ties [13, 14].
Adjacent pairs in the top 20 get paired bootstrap z-tests with Holm's correction [15].

**Power.** For #1 and each of the next ten complete-coverage models we compute the per-unit paired differences and
the standard error $\widehat{se}_0$ of their group-balanced mean. A true gain $\Delta$ is detected with 80% power by
a two-sided test at $\alpha = 0.05$ with $n = n_0 [(z_{0.975} + z_{0.8}) \widehat{se}_0 / \Delta]^2$ units,
assuming new assays arrive as new units in today's function mix (217/200 assays per unit) [11, 12]. We check the
formula by resampling the real paired differences re-centred at $\Delta$.

**Coverage simulation.** We simulate $X_{um} = \mu_{g(u)m} + a_u + \varepsilon_{um}$ on the real unit and group
structure for the 96 complete-coverage models: $\mu$ from the observed group means, unit effects $a_u$ resampled,
and residual rows resampled from the data (keeping cross-model correlation and heavy tails) or drawn Gaussian with
the empirical covariance. Scenarios: calibrated, Gaussian, exact ties (top five tied and ranks 20–29 tied), near
ties (top five 0.002 apart) and separated (true gaps ×3), each with 1,000 synthetic leaderboards and 1,000
bootstrap replicates. A tied model's interval counts as covering only if it contains every rank the model can
take, which directly tests the failure under ties reported in [2].

## 4. Results

**Table 1.** Coverage simulation: worst-model coverage of the true rank (nominal 0.95), range of joint coverage
over the five scenarios, and mean width of the top-10 intervals (calibrated scenario, ranks).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bootstrap percentile | 0.845 | 0.873 | 0.081 | 0.888 | 0.855 | 0.00–0.28 | 5.1 |
| Marginal, single-step | 0.995 | 0.997 | 0.973 | 0.996 | 0.992 | 0.89–0.98 | 10.4 |
| Marginal, step-down | 0.982 | 0.971 | 0.909 | 0.971 | 0.977 | 0.69–0.87 | 8.0 |
| Marginal, bootstrap-t | 0.982 | 0.977 | 0.940 | 0.975 | 0.980 | 0.79–0.92 | 9.8 |
| Simultaneous, step-down | 0.997 | 0.998 | 0.994 | 0.999 | 0.997 | 0.98–1.00 | 13.8 |

**The intervals are valid (Table 1).** Marginal single-step and simultaneous step-down intervals keep worst-model
coverage at or above 0.973 in every scenario. The price is width: the top-10 intervals span 10.4 and 13.8 ranks on
average, against 5.1 for the percentile interval. The percentile interval drops to 0.081 for exactly tied models.
Marginal step-down under-covers under exact ties (0.909; 0.940 when studentised), so we do not use it for headline
numbers. The best-model set contains a single true #1 in at least 99.7% of runs. With a five-way tie at #1 it
contains each tied model 98.1% and all five 93.1% of the time, compared with 75.6% for reading ProteinGym's error
bars as "within 1.96 SE of #1".

**Ranks are uncertain (Fig. 1a).** AIDO Protein-RAG and VenusREM differ by 0.00004, and each is #1 in about half
of the bootstrap replicates (49% and 50%). The 95% best-model set is AIDO Protein-RAG, VenusREM and ProSST (K=4096)
(the naive reading gives AIDO Protein-RAG, VenusREM and ProSST (K=2048)). Only 2 of 19 adjacent pairs in the top 20
differ significantly (VenusREM > ProSST (K=2048), z = 4.9; S3F-MSA > S2F-MSA, z = 5.9), with or without Holm's
correction. The marginal interval for #4 is ranks 1–13 and for #10 6–28; the simultaneous intervals extend to 18
and 42. The gap between #1 and #3 is 0.011 (SE 0.0063, z = 1.8).

**The benchmark is underpowered at the top (Fig. 1b).** With 80% power the benchmark detects a gain of about 0.020
over #1 (0.015–0.027 across the ten pairs). Detecting 0.01 needs about 780 units (450–1,460), or about 850 assays,
3.9× today; 0.005 needs about 3,120 units (1,810–5,830), or 3,380 assays, 15.6× today. Resampling agrees with the
formula to within 0.05 in power, and the test's size is 6.0% at today's size and 9.4% at a quarter of it. These
figures are for one pairwise comparison; claiming a new #1 against every other model needs a multiplicity
correction and more assays still.

**The top depends on the metric and the aggregation.** With 4,000 replicates per leaderboard: under Spearman, AUC
and MCC with any of four mean-based aggregations (ProteinGym's, function-group mean, protein-weighted, flat), the
possible-#1 set stays within AIDO Protein-RAG, ProSST (K=4096) and VenusREM; only the name in the #1 slot changes
(VenusREM leads under the function-group mean). Dropping the Activity or OrganismalFitness assays makes VenusREM
#1. NDCG ranks different models: S3F-MSA is #1, only 4 of the published top 10 stay in its top 10 (Kendall
τ = 0.65–0.70) and 4–6 models are possible #1s. Top-K recall leaves 9–12 possible #1s, and the median Spearman 12.
This echoes the general point that aggregate leaderboards are sensitive to seemingly minor design choices [9, 10].

**Unequal assay coverage biases the ranking.** Protriever (#8) is scored on 200 of 217 assays, and it is the only
incomplete model. Its 17 missing assays (4 Activity, 3 Expression, 9 OrganismalFitness, 1 Stability) are harder
for every other model (mean Spearman 0.379 against 0.410), so skipping them flatters it. On the 200 common assays
it falls to #10 and VenusREM becomes #1.

**Per-assay inputs replicate.** Re-scoring five small assays (one per function group) with ESM-2 8M–650M [19] on
CPU, using ProteinGym's masked-marginal scoring [20], reproduces 20/20 published Spearman values at 3 dp (maximum
difference 0.0005; 2.4e-6 against ProteinGym's own per-mutant scores) in 4.3 minutes. Scoring choices are as large
as leaderboard gaps: wild-type marginals move Spearman by up to 0.133 (median 0.016), and pooling double mutants
raises TCRG1 (650M) from 0.719 on singles to 0.769. Within-assay mutant-bootstrap SEs are 0.010–0.085, and 7 of 20
steps up in model size lower Spearman (1 significantly). The rank uncertainty above therefore comes from which
proteins are sampled, not from irreproducible scores.

## 5. Limitations

- **Sampling model.** Proteins are treated as exchangeable draws within function groups. ProteinGym's assays are
  curated, not sampled, so the intervals describe re-curation of similar proteins, not a different curation policy.
- **Unmodelled noise.** Mutant-level measurement noise, scoring choices and training randomness are not
  propagated, so the intervals understate total uncertainty. Per-assay scores are rounded to 3 dp.
- **Small strata.** Binding has 12 units and Expression 18; bootstrap critical values are approximate there, which
  shows as the mildly liberal test size and the step-down under-coverage above.
- **Conservative intervals.** Single-step intervals over-cover (mean coverage at least 0.998 against 0.95), so the
  widths are an upper end.
- **Power assumptions.** A new model is assumed to correlate with #1 as today's challengers do, and new assays to
  resemble current ones.
- **Scope.** Protriever's missing block looks like a processing gap but is unconfirmed. The ESM-2 check covers five
  short assays up to 650M parameters and does not exercise long-sequence windowing. This note is not peer reviewed,
  and the novelty statement rests on a literature search of 6 October 2026.

## 6. Proposed change for ProteinGym

`performance_DMS_benchmarks.py` already draws 10,000 stratified bootstrap replicates for its error bars. From the
same replicates, at a cost of seconds and with no new data, it could:

1. add to the leaderboard a marginal 95% rank interval, a simultaneous 95% rank interval and a "possible #1" flag
   (the 95% best-model set);
2. rank models that skip assays on the common subset, or show their coverage next to their rank;
3. state the minimum detectable difference (about 0.020 today) on the leaderboard page, so that new models are not
   reported as state of the art on smaller differences.

`pgnoise` implements all three and could be offered as a pull request.
