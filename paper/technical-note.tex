\title{\vspace{-1.2cm}\bfseries\large Can ProteinGym scores support a model decision?\\
\normalsize Power, rank uncertainty and assay-design sensitivity on the zero-shot DMS benchmark}
\author{Nissan Dutta\\\small Technical note, draft of October 2026. Code and data provenance:
\url{https://github.com/Nissan-Dutta/genesis}}
\date{\vspace{-0.9cm}}

\maketitle

\noindent Decisions about AI models in biology rest on evaluation scores. On the field's main public protein benchmark,
the smallest gain reliably detectable at the top is about \mddAtTop{} (80\% power), larger than the gap between the
published \#1 and \#2 and comparable to the gap to \#3. Which model ranks \#1 also depends on which kinds of lab tests
are counted---a design choice, not a property of the models. We ask how large an evaluation must be before its numbers
can support a decision.

\noindent\textbf{Summary.} At commit \pgCommit{}, \nModels{} models and \nAssays{} assays, today's benchmark could
support claims about gains of roughly \mddMed{} Spearman or more over the leader (\mddMin--\mddMax{} across ten pairs);
$\Delta=0.01$ would need about \assaysTenMed{} assays (\timesTenMed$\times$ today). Rank intervals leave \nBestSet{}
models not ruled out as \#1; only \nNeighbourSig{} of 19 adjacent top-20 pairs differ significantly. Shared-assay
re-ranking makes \topAllCommon{} \#1 on \nCommonAssays{} assays (vs \topPublished{} on all assays). We treat leaderboard
scores as a \emph{hypothesis} of preferability for mutation-effect prediction, not evidence of dangerous capability.

\section{Question}
ProteinGym~\cite{notin2023proteingym} reports bootstrap SEs of each model's gap to \#1, not rank uncertainty.
Neuhof and Benjamini~\cite{neuhof2026rank} give rank intervals for TabArena and MMLU; we apply that style of max-$t$
inference and power analysis to ProteinGym's nested structure. Woolley et al.~\cite{woolley2026overestimating} warn
that aggregates hide dataset-level variance; Livesey and Marsh~\cite{livesey2023updated,livesey2025variant} bootstrap
rank significance on their own DMS benchmarks. To our knowledge, this is the first simultaneous rank-interval and
power analysis of the ProteinGym DMS leaderboard with assay-subset sensitivity; we do not claim the first rank
intervals ever~\cite{neuhof2026rank,chiang2024chatbot}.

\section{Data and version}
We use ProteinGym GitHub commit \pgCommit{} (25 March 2026): release v1.3 plus AIDO Protein-RAG and Protriever,
\nModels{} models and \nAssays{} assays. As in ProteinGym's script, assay scores are averaged within
(UniProt ID, function) units (\nUnits{} units), units within five function groups (Activity, Binding, Expression,
OrganismalFitness, Stability), and the score is the mean of the five group means. We reproduce \nExactAverage{}/\nModels{} averages and \nExactRank{}/\nModels{} ranks; \nExactSE{}/\nModels{} SEs match
at 3\,dp. We report overall statistics only and do not stratify by organism or viral proteins, as a
biosecurity-adjacent judgment rather than a data limitation.

\section{Methods}
\textbf{Estimand.} Let $\theta_m$ be model $m$'s ProteinGym aggregate in the limit of infinitely many units per
function group, treating the benchmark's units as a sample from each group. Its rank is
$r_m = 1 + \#\{k:\theta_k>\theta_m\}$. Our intervals quantify the uncertainty due to which proteins happen to be in
the benchmark. They are not prediction intervals for a model's rank on a \emph{new} assay, which is Neuhof and
Benjamini's target and is much wider.

\textbf{Bootstrap.} Exactly as for ProteinGym's error bars, units are resampled with replacement within function
groups ($B=\nBoot$), a stratified bootstrap in the sense of~\cite{agarwal2021deep}. Missing scores are skipped, as in
ProteinGym.

\textbf{Rank intervals.} Following Mogstad et al.~\cite{mogstad2024inference}, let
$T_{jk}=(\hat\theta_j-\hat\theta_k-\theta_j+\theta_k)/\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{jk}$. The \emph{marginal} interval
for model $j$ takes $c_j$, the 95\% bootstrap quantile of $\max_k|T^*_{jk}|$, and sets the lower bound to
$1+\#\{k:\hat\theta_k-\hat\theta_j>c_j\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{jk}\}$ and the upper bound to
$p-\#\{k:\hat\theta_j-\hat\theta_k>c_j\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{jk}\}$; it covers $r_j$ with probability at least 95\%
for each $j$. The \emph{simultaneous} interval uses one critical value from $\max_{j,k}|T^*_{jk}|$ and covers all
ranks jointly; its step-down version~\cite{romano2005exact} recomputes the critical value over the comparisons not
yet rejected. The 95\% best-model set keeps $j$ unless some model beats it at a one-sided max-$t$ critical value, so
it contains the true \#1 with probability at least 95\%. We report marginal single-step and simultaneous step-down
intervals. The percentile interval of bootstrap ranks is shown for contrast; it has no guarantee and fails near
ties~\cite{hall2009bootstrap,almohamad2022simultaneous}. Adjacent pairs in the top 20 get paired bootstrap $z$-tests
with Holm's correction~\cite{holm1979simple}.

\textbf{Power.} For \#1 and each of the next ten complete-coverage models we compute the per-unit paired
differences and the standard error $\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_0$ of their group-balanced mean. A true gain $\Delta$ is
detected with 80\% power by a two-sided test at $\alpha=0.05$ with
$n=n_0\,[(z_{0.975}+z_{0.8})\,\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_0/\Delta]^2$ units, assuming new assays arrive as new units in
today's function mix (\nAssays/\nUnits{} assays per unit)~\cite{card2020little,miller2024adding}. We check the
formula by resampling the real paired differences re-centred at $\Delta$.

\textbf{Coverage simulation.} Five scenarios, \simReps{} synthetic leaderboards each (\simBoot{} bootstrap replicates),
as in the repository; tied ranks require set coverage~\cite{neuhof2026rank}.

\section{Results}
\textbf{Power (Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}a).} With 80\% power at today's \nUnits{} units, the median MDD over \#1 vs the
next ten is \mddMed{} (\mddMin--\mddMax{}); the best pair is \mddAtTop{}, above the \gapOneTwo{} gap between \topOne{}
and \topTwo{} and near the \gapOneThree{} gap to \#3. $\Delta=0.01$ needs $\approx$\assaysTenMed{} assays
(\timesTenMed$\times$); $\Delta=0.005$ needs $\approx$\assaysFiveMed{} (\timesFiveMed$\times$). We phrase ``this gap
supports a decision'' as a hypothesis at stated $\Delta$, not a \#1 verdict.

\textbf{(A) Statistical uncertainty (Fig.~\ref{fig:headline}b).} \topOne{} and \topTwo{} each lead in $\approx$\pRankOneTop\%
and \pRankOneSecond\% of bootstrap draws; the 95\% best-model set is \bestSet{}. Only \nNeighbourSig{} adjacent top-20
pairs differ significantly. Marginal intervals keep worst-model coverage $\ge$\minMargWorst{} in simulation;
simultaneous step-down $\ge$\minSimulWorst{}; naive percentile ranks drop to \covPctTies{} under exact ties.

\textbf{(B) Assay mix and averaging (design).} Dropping \logoFlipGroups{} makes \logoFlipLeader{} \#1. On all
\nCommonAssays{} assays scored by every model, \topAllCommon{} leads (vs \topPublished{} on all \nAssays{}); the
possible-\#1 set is \bestSetAllCommon{} (Table~\ref{tab:shared}). The published top-$K{=}10$ share the same
\nTopTenCommonAssays{} assays here. NDCG reorders sharply (\ndcgOverlap{}/10 of the published top 10 remain).
Protriever (\#\protRank{}) falls to \#\protRankCommon{} on shared assays; its missing assays average \othersOnMissing{}
vs \othersOnCommon{} for others.

\section{Limitations}
Intervals omit mutant-level noise; power assumes future assays resemble today's mix. High mutation-effect scores are
not evidence of misuse potential. Literature search 6 October 2026.
